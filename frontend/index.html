THE SCIENTIFIC CHALLENGE

Why Detecting Environmental Trends Matters

Planetary ecosystems are undergoing unprecedented transformations. Separating short-term weather noise from statistically significant multi-decadal climate trends is critical for environmental policy, resource stewardship, and community resilience.

Weather Noise vs. True Trends

Natural inter-annual oscillations like El Niño and seasonal cycles can obscure underlying multi-decadal warming or drying trends. TerraTrend applies rigorous non-parametric Mann-Kendall tests to identify genuine directional shifts.

Spatial Heterogeneity

Earth's climate changes unevenly. While the Arctic experiences rapid polar amplification warming, other sectors endure intensified drought or cloudburst volatility. Pinpointing exact geographic boundaries is essential.

Actionable Confidence

Policymakers need more than raw curves—they need quantifiable confidence intervals, annual rates of change, and plain-language interpretations to safeguard food security, urban infrastructure, and fresh water.

Powered by NASA Satellite Missions & Models

TerraTrend is designed to interface with world-class Earth science missions operated by NASA and international partners.

MODIS

Terra & Aqua Satellites

36 spectral bands providing global daily imagery of land surface temperature, vegetation indices (NDVI), and aerosol optical depth.

GSFC • 2000 - Present • 250m to 1km resolution
GPM

Precipitation Measurement

Dual-frequency radar and multi-satellite IMERG synthesis delivering global 3-hour liquid and frozen precipitation observations.

NASA / JAXA • 2014 - Present • 0.1° (~10km)
AIRS

Atmospheric Infrared Sounder

High-spectral resolution infrared spectrometer aboard Aqua measuring 3D atmospheric temperature, moisture, and greenhouse gas columns.

NASA JPL • 2002 - Present • 1° gridded
OMI

Ozone Monitoring Instrument

UV-Visible spectrometer on Aura tracking stratospheric ozone layer thickness, UV index levels, and atmospheric trace pollutants (NO2, SO2).

NASA / KNMI • 2004 - Present • 13x24 km
MERRA-2
